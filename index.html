Questions parents ask.

Isn't a Rubik's cube just a toy?
It looks like one. To solve it, a child memorises patterns, plans moves ahead and holds their focus for minutes at a time, which is exactly what homework asks of them. That's why parents notice the change outside cubing too.
My child isn't a maths kid. Will this work?
You don't need to be good at maths. Every cube is broken into small steps, and a coach walks them through each one. Most children get their first solve in the first week or two.
What age is this for?
Divya coaches children from [age] upwards. Batches are grouped by level, so younger kids never feel behind.
Won't an online class mean more screen time?
Scrolling is hours of switching every few seconds. Our class is one hour of the opposite: hands on a cube, eyes on one problem, with a coach watching. Many parents find the cube starts replacing the phone in free time.
My child starts things and drops them. Why would this be different?
Kids drop things when there's no next level. Here there's a new cube, a new trophy and a Cube Clash every month, and our promise means we don't let go if they're doing the work.
Can't they just learn from YouTube for free?
They can try. But YouTube lives on the same phone we're trying to get them off, there's no roadmap, and nobody helps when they're stuck. Most kids stop at the first layer.
What do we need at home?
A laptop, tablet or phone with Zoom, and a quiet spot for an hour. We send the first cube to your door.
How much does it cost?
Programs run from 3 to 12 months. On a short call we'll look at where your child is today and where you'd love them to be, and recommend the right one. During a scholarship season, every family who applies gets something.
